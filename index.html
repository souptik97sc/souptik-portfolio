I love the moment a complicated problem starts to make sense, especially when you can see the relief on someone’s face.

Today I work in Enterprise Customer Success at Okta, helping customers navigate identity and security. 02 / My philosophy

Why Customer Success?

Because the best customer work isn’t reactive. It helps customers make sense of where they are, what’s in the way, and what they should tackle next.

For me, Customer Success isn’t just about keeping customers happy. It’s about understanding what they’re trying to achieve, becoming the right support system around that ambition, and helping create scalable, sustainable growth for the customer and the company, one meaningful challenge at a time.

01

Customer value first.

Commercial growth matters to me. I believe it is earned by helping customers get better results first.

02

Read the signal, not just the dashboard.

Usage data matters, but so do repeated words, stakeholder hesitation, competitive anxiety, and quiet hints about what is not quite working yet.

03

Make the hard thing feel workable.

I like making complexity usable: breaking down technical or organisational challenges into something practical, paced, and achievable.

03 / My approach

How I tend to work with customers.

This is the rhythm behind most of my work, whether I’m helping an account grow, stabilising a difficult relationship, or guiding customers toward better value realisation.

01 / Listen closely

Start with context.

Understand the history, the original buying decision, the people involved, and the current business pressure before recommending anything new.

02 / Find the signal

Look for what matters.

Sometimes it’s in adoption data. Sometimes it’s in a repeated comment like “ROI” or a quiet concern about a future gap.

03 / Make it practical

Reduce the sprawl.

Turn complexity into something the customer can realistically act on, whether that’s a learning plan, a workshop, or a focused success path.

04 / Bring people together

Coordinate the right expertise.

Good Customer Success often means orchestrating product, engineering, sales, renewals, leadership, and customer stakeholders around the same problem.

05 / Keep one eye ahead

Think beyond today.

I like helping customers solve the present problem while preparing for the next one. That is where retention, expansion, and advocacy usually start to take shape.

A little lesson from experience: More meetings do not automatically mean more value. Sometimes the best thing you can do is give someone a focused 30 minute learning plan and let them get on with their day.

Case study 01 / Growth

Beyond Adoption.

Turning a mature enterprise relationship into sustainable account growth.

+

The opportunity

I inherited a mature enterprise customer with strong adoption, a highly technical team, and a stable deployment. The challenge wasn’t rescue. It was identifying what additional value could be unlocked without forcing an expansion agenda.

What I did

I rebuilt stakeholder context, ran structured feature and exposure assessments, and shifted the conversation from current deployment to future readiness. I coordinated product, technical, and commercial teams to support demos, evaluations, and collaborative product feedback.

What changed

  • Closed a smaller expansion for additional users and capacity.
  • Developed a larger, approved budget expansion opportunity pending signature.
  • Deepened adoption of existing capabilities and grew customer advocacy.
My takeaway: strong adoption is a milestone, not the destination. Some of the best expansion opportunities come from helping successful customers imagine what’s possible next.
Case study 02 / Retention

When the Champion Leaves.

Rebuilding operational confidence, reducing P1 incidents, and protecting a strategic customer relationship.

+

The challenge

An established customer lost its principal platform administrator with little knowledge transfer. New owners inherited a complex environment, critical issues increased, and confidence in the relationship came under pressure during a strategically sensitive period.

What I did

I worked with the new stakeholders and internal technical teams to unpick critical business workflows, then designed a personalised learning plan requiring only 30 to 45 minutes per week. I also created a coordinated recovery path with Sales, Renewals, leadership, and Customer Success.

What changed

  • P1 incidents reduced from approximately 6 to 7 per week to zero across a subsequent month.
  • A material churn risk was removed through a successful renewal.
  • The customer moved from a previous one year term to a three year renewal.
My takeaway: the real problem was not just technical. It was lost knowledge, reduced confidence, and limited capacity. Great retention work solves the human and operational challenge behind the incident.
Case study 03 / Advisory

The Value Behind the Metrics.

Two examples of consultative Customer Success: value realisation and executive strategy.

+

03A · ROI and feature value

With a technically mature FSI customer, I kept hearing one phrase: “return on investment.” Even though product usage looked healthy, I led an in person Feature Adoption Assessment to explore whether they were using the most relevant capabilities within what they already owned. That work helped surface optimisation opportunities and created stronger trust for future capability discussions.

03B · Benchmarking for executives

With a large banking customer, I used a leadership transition as a moment to elevate the conversation. I translated internal adoption intelligence and industry patterns into a strategic workshop that spoke to what the CISO cared about most: competitive positioning, technology direction, and future challenges.

Why it matters

  • Not every healthy account needs a product pitch.
  • Sometimes customers first need help seeing value more clearly.
  • And sometimes executives need strategic context, not another dashboard.
My takeaway: consultative Customer Success means adapting the conversation to what the customer actually values, whether that is ROI, operational confidence, or strategic direction.
